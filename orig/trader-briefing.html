— PRE-SESSION BRIEFING

How we found these zones

What the system does, what it claims, and what we want from your eyes.

What you're looking at

The TradingView script you'll load shows 28 supply & demand zones that an automated detector pulled out of two years of Binance and Bybit perpetual data. Each zone is a price area where the chart showed a tight consolidation (a "base") followed by an impulsive move out of that base. Classic supply/demand logic, programmed and run at scale.

These aren't random zones. They're a curated selection — the ones the system has the strongest opinions about, both positive and negative. Your job is to tell me whether the system's opinions match what you'd actually trade.

The exact spec — how zones were found

Timeframe
H1 (1-hour candles). Zones are detected on the hourly chart. You can flip to any timeframe in TradingView once a zone is on screen — the rectangles stay anchored to the H1 detection time.
Forward window
The system measures what happened in the 24 hours after the zone formed (24 × H1 bars). Each zone is then classified as a win, loss, no_touch, or chop based on whether price hit a placeholder target or stop within that window.
Base detection
A "base" is 2–8 consecutive H1 candles where the total range stays within 1.5× ATR(14). Tight, but lenient by design — we want the proposer to surface candidates, not pre-filter them.
Leg-out detection
The candle immediately after the base must have a body bigger than 0.8× ATR(14) and close beyond the base bounds. That's the impulsive move that gives the zone its directional bias.
Pattern types
All four classical S&D variants — see panel below.
Asset universe
BTCUSDT.P, ETHUSDT.P, SOLUSDT.P on both Binance and Bybit. Funding rate, multiple EMAs, RSI, ATR percentile, and time-of-day all logged at zone formation.
Detection window
Trained the scoring models on 2024-01 → 2025-06. The zones you'll see in the script are from 2025-07 onwards — out-of-sample, the model never trained on them.

The four pattern types

How confident we are

— THE HONEST ANSWER
The system works in a two-stage way, and we have different confidence in each stage.

Stage 1 — Predicting which zones resolve cleanly within 4 hours

This is the system's strongest signal. Across BTC, ETH, and SOL, it correctly predicts which zones will hit a clean win or loss within 4 hours about 71% of the time on out-of-sample data. The top decile of "will resolve" zones actually resolves 52.5% of the time; the bottom decile only 3.0%. That's a real, calibrated separation.

Stage 2 — Among zones that resolve, predicting win vs loss

Weaker, and asset-dependent. On ETH specifically, when we filter to zones the resolution model flagged as "will resolve" and look at the top decile of "this will win" predictions, the model is right about 76% of the time on out-of-sample data. On BTC the equivalent number is roughly 67% but with wide error bars. On SOL it doesn't work at all — basically random.

What this means in plain terms

  • The system can identify "easy call" zones across all three assets (Stage 1 works).
  • Among those easy calls, it picks winners well on ETH, somewhat on BTC, poorly on SOL.
  • The signal only holds at short horizons (4h forward, sometimes 8h). At 24h, signal vanishes.
  • None of this accounts for fees, slippage, or real execution. These are correlations on historical data, not P&L.

What we want from this session

The TradingView script will show you 28 zones grouped into three panels, color-coded:

PANEL 1 — BLUE
Does the "easy call" filter match your eye?
12 zones — 6 the system flags as "will resolve cleanly within 4h," 6 it flags as "won't resolve." Outcomes are hidden from you. Just tell us whether each one looks like a setup you would trade, regardless of how it played out. We're testing whether the system's "easy" matches your "I'd take that."
PANEL 2 — GREEN
Can you rank ETH zones by likelihood of winning?
8 ETH zones, all from the "will resolve" pool, presented in scrambled order. Your task: rank them 1 (most likely winner) to 8 (least likely). Outcomes hidden until you submit. We'll compare your ranking to the model's and to what actually happened. This tests whether the model's strongest signal lines up with your judgment.
PANEL 3 — RED
Why did the model fail here?
8 zones (4 BTC, 4 SOL) where the model predicted a win but the trade actually lost. Outcomes shown from the start. Free-text question: what did the model miss? Real setup that failed for an external reason? Or did the model misjudge the setup itself?

Important — please don't do these

  • Don't try to back-figure the model's prediction from the score we show you. The number on each zone in panels 1 and 2 is the resolution probability — it's a property of the zone, not a "we think this will win" claim. Treat it as background.
  • Don't look up the outcome elsewhere before you submit your panel 1 and 2 answers. The redaction is the point. Once you've submitted, the script will reveal everything.
  • Don't optimize your answers for what you think we want to hear. If a zone looks bad to you, mark it bad. If panel 2's ranking feels random because all 8 zones look similar, say so — that's a valid signal too.

What we'll do with your answers

After the session, we'll know one of three things:

  • Your judgment lines up with the model on the strong-confidence zones → we have a candidate signal worth validating with real money on paper trades first.
  • Your judgment disagrees with the model in specific ways → the disagreement tells us what features the model is missing. We'd add those features and retrain.
  • Your judgment is mixed — agreement on some zones, disagreement on others, no clear pattern → we'd narrow the scope (maybe only ETH, maybe only certain pattern types) and try again.

All three outcomes are useful. None of them require the session to "go well" in any particular direction.

— ONE LAST THING
The system has run three rounds of validation on its own findings, and three different headline claims have been retired when they didn't survive testing. What's in the script is what's left after that process. The point of bringing it to you isn't to validate that we're right. It's to find out what we're missing.
