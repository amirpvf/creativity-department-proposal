System Brief / v0.1
Internal Document — Trader Working Group
Behavioral Infrastructure for Discretionary Trading

The trader
already knows.
The system
helps them act.

A pre-commitment system that turns a trader's implicit edge into an explicit, enforceable contract — and intervenes at the moment of deviation, not the morning after.

§ 01
The Gap

Mistakes are known before they're made.
No tool exists at the moment of decision.

Traders repeat the same handful of errors across years of experience. They aren't knowledge gaps — they're behavioral gaps. The trader sees the mistake forming and clicks anyway.

Tool Class A

Journals

Edgewonk, Tradezella, spreadsheets. Manual entry, post-session review. Insight arrives hours after the trade is closed and the damage is done.

→ Past tense
Tool Class B

Platforms

TradingView, NinjaTrader, broker terminals. Real-time execution but stateless — no memory of the trader's prior decisions, patterns, or losses.

→ Present tense, no memory
Tool Class C

Predictive AI

Signal services, pattern detectors, sentiment dashboards. Predicts the market — a problem the trader doesn't actually need help with — and ignores the trader entirely.

→ Wrong subject

The closed loop between what the trader learns after the fact and what the trader does in the moment does not exist in any product on the market. That gap is the wedge.

§ 02
The Reframe

Don't predict the market.
Predict the trader.

Modeling a single human's edge and deviations from it is dramatically more tractable than modeling the market — and dramatically more valuable, because for experienced discretionary traders, execution discipline is the dominant source of P&L variance.

Best months and worst months often share similar win rates. The difference is the magnitude and frequency of mistakes — three to five trades a month that turn green to red. The system's job is to prevent those specific trades, not to find new ones.

Traders don't pay for signals.
They pay to stop losing money.

Operating Principle
§ 03
Core Mechanism

A closed learning loop, not a feature set.

Seven steps that compound. Each step generates the data that improves the next. The kill switch — the trader's escape hatch — is the highest-quality signal in the system.

Diagram 01 / The Loop

The kill switch is not a UX concession. Every time it is pressed, the system records what fired, what the trader did instead, and what happened. This becomes the disagreement corpus — the highest-quality labeled data for distinguishing genuine edge expression from rationalization.

§ 04
Edge Representation

The trader has three edges.
The gaps between them are the product.

Diagram 02 / The Three Edges
A — Stated
What the trader believes their edge is. Almost always cleaner and more disciplined than reality. The version they would describe in an interview.
B — Revealed
What the trade record actually shows when their winning clusters are isolated. Often narrower and weirder than stated — different time of day, different setup variant, different market conditions.
C — Enforced
The intersection of A and B, weighted by recency and confidence. This is what the watchdog uses. The trader pre-commits to it during calm review — not in the heat of the session.

The gap between A and B is where the "this tool understands me" moment lives. Surfacing it once, accurately, in month one is what converts a trial into a long-term user.

§ 05
Intervention Design

Friction over notification.
Mirror, not judge.

Notifications are ignored within days. Friction reshapes behavior. A two-second hold-to-confirm with a personally relevant statistic is more powerful than any alert because it manufactures a single conscious moment between impulse and action.

— Weak
"You've lost $2,140 doing this."
⊕ Better
"You've done this 23 times. 4 worked."
★ Strongest
"This matches your losing pattern from the last 2 sessions."

Dollar amounts get rationalized. Frequency and base rates resist rationalization. Temporal proximity — "the last 2 sessions" — is hardest of all, because the trader can recall the specific trades and the rationalization collapses.

No labels like "tilt" or "bad trade." No authority tone. Only patterns, probabilities, and comparisons. The system is a mirror — the trader makes the call.

§ 06
Foundation

Capture is the moat.
Everything downstream compounds from it.

If capture is wrong, the model is garbage and the alerts are noise. If capture is right, even crude downstream logic produces value. This is the only place in the stack where 99% reliability is non-negotiable.

Diagram 03 / Event Taxonomy

Every event carries a timestamp, sequence number, and provenance flag — what produced it, with what confidence. The system trusts no single source. Sessions reconcile to broker statements at end-of-day; sessions that fail reconciliation are excluded from training data, never silently consumed.

§ 07
Non-Stationarity

Edge drifts. The system must drift with it.

A model trained on six months of trades will silently degrade. February's A+ setup becomes August's B-minus. A static system flags good trades as deviations and burns trust on each false positive. The fix is structural, not parametric.

Rolling priors
Behavioral patterns learned over a sliding window. Older data decays in influence rather than holding equal weight to last week.
Self-expiring rules
Each rule has a trigger count, override rate, and outcome correlation. High overrides with good outcomes weaken the rule. Low usage decays it. No static rules.
Confidence gating
The system intervenes only when pattern stability is high and recent validation holds. Otherwise it stays silent. Silence is a feature.
Regime detection
Sudden drops in pattern accuracy or rising override rates trigger recalibration mode — fewer interventions, more reflection prompts. The system tells the trader "your behavior has shifted, let's relabel."
§ 08
Trust Sequence

Trust is sequenced.
You earn it before you spend it.

Diagram 04 / Trust Ladder

The Enforcer is not the system imposing control. It is the trader pre-committing to lockouts during calm review — a Ulysses contract — that the system mechanically enforces during the session. The trader is restricting themselves; the tool is the mechanism. This framing also keeps the product on the user's side of the broker API and out of regulatory hot water.

§ 09
The Escape Hatch

The kill switch is the most valuable signal in the system.

A single keystroke disables all interventions for the rest of the session, no questions asked. Counterintuitive but essential — traders will not adopt a tool they fear in a real moment of need.

Every press logs which rule fired, what the trader did instead, and what happened next. This is the disagreement corpus: clean labels for "system was about to be right and I overrode it" versus "system was wrong and got in my way." After each press, the system optionally prompts a ten-second voice memo. Half will skip. The other half generate the highest-quality training data in the entire pipeline, captured at peak emotional salience.

§ 10
Build Sequence

Four phases.
Earn the right to be real-time.

The first eight weeks produce nothing visibly impressive. This is by design. Capture and reflection have to be correct in hindsight before any real-time intervention can be trusted.

PHASE 0
CaptureWeeks 1–2
Perfect passive capture. No UX. No insights. No AI. Raw timeline of every session — orders, intents, stops, snapshots, market context. Success criterion: 99%+ event reliability and full broker reconciliation.
PHASE 1
ReconstructionWeeks 3–4
Auto-generated trade timeline with replay. Light rule-based flags only — stop moved, trade after loss, trade near news. No interpretation. Success criterion: the trader can review a session in 10 minutes and recognize every event.
PHASE 2
Reflection EngineWeeks 4–6
Five-second per-trade structured prompts: was this part of your A+ setup? Did you follow your plan? What went wrong? The trader teaches the model. Success criterion: a personalized labeled dataset that did not exist before.
PHASE 3
First Real-Time GuardrailWeeks 6–8
Two or three rules promoted to real-time, with two-second hold-to-confirm and personal stat surfacing. Success criterion: measurable behavior change on at least one rule, with honest attribution. No new rule until an existing one proves itself.
§ 11
Operating Discipline

The project succeeds on restraint,
not on intelligence.

— 01
No new real-time rule until an existing one proves measurable behavior change.
— 02
Every rule must adapt or die. No static rules. Override-driven decay is built in from day one.
— 03
Silence is a feature. The system intervenes only when pattern stability is high.
— 04
Facts in the canonical layer. Interpretations downstream. Never embed a definition you might want to change later.
— 05
No ML beyond simple stats for the first 6–8 weeks. Earn the model with the data.
— 06
One trader. One behavior changed. That is the test that decides whether the project is real.
§ 12
North Star Metric

Not retention. Not usage.
Did the system surface something true?

The aha moment is structural: the system shows the trader a pattern in their behavior they recognize as true but had not articulated. Specific. Asymmetric. Recognizable as theirs. One of these in the first month converts a trial into a long-term user. Zero converts to a cancellation.

Earn the right to be real-time
by being correct in hindsight first.

Operating Rule

If post-session reconstruction is not accurate, useful, and trusted, real-time intervention will fail. There is no shortcut. The capture layer is the moat. The reflection loop is the asset. The watchdog is the visible product. The kill switch is the truth.
